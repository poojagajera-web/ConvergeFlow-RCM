## Which medical revenue cycle management questions come first?

Start with your practice’s own patient-to-payment workflow. Identify the information needed before appointments, the charge and claim questions that need review, and the responsibilities for payment and balance follow-up. Use that map to distinguish essential requirements from optional ones. A broad module name does not confirm support for a particular medical specialty or transaction.

---

## What information should you ask to see?

Ask to see the actual fields and responses available for your medical use case. The observed verification queue combines appointment, insurance and task context; the eligibility view includes review flags. Confirm the payer and service-specific information your staff needs, how incomplete responses appear and which decisions remain with your established review process.

---

## How should you review billing and follow-up?

Review the available billing and receivables views against your claim and balance workflows. Ask which actions are supported, where records originate and how status changes reach the next reviewer. The presence of a submission queue or ERA/EOB area does not validate claim submission or payment posting. Keep those actions open questions until they are demonstrated for your requirements.

---

## Who should join the fit discussion?

Include the people who own operational, system and reporting requirements. Bring your PMS or EHR name, the payer questions your team handles and a description of the handoffs you want to examine. Discuss authorization, coding and other service-specific needs explicitly. Do not send patient identifiers, insurance documents or clinical information through the public request form.
