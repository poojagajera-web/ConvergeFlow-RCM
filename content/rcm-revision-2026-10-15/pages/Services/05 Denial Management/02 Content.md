## Describe the issue before choosing an action

Begin a denial management workflow with the information actually received, rather than an assumed cause. Preserve the relevant response and its source in your approved record. Identify which question the next reviewer must answer before deciding how your practice should proceed.

## Give unresolved work an owner

A review record is useful when it explains what is known, what has been checked and who follows up. Separate a source-data question from a billing decision or a system issue. Establish a next action and review trigger through your practice process instead of leaving the record under an unexplained pending label.

## Questions about denial management

### Does ConvergeFlow automatically appeal denials?

Automated appeals have not been confirmed. This page explains a review workflow and does not promise denial recovery, payer contact or appeal submission. Ask which actions are available for your use case, who performs each action and what evidence is provided before planning to use the product for follow-up.

### Will a denial view explain every cause?

Do not assume that it will. A response or status must be read with its source and available detail. Ask what information is visible, which questions still require investigation and how your team can record the next action. Product evaluation should distinguish displayed information from a confirmed explanation of an issue.

### What does a useful review scenario include?

Use a de-identified description of the response, the information your staff checked and the question still open. Identify the person responsible for the next decision. Ask to see the available product context and actions, including what happens when the information is insufficient to proceed. Keep patient documents off the marketing form.

### Can the product promise recovery or fewer denials?

No such outcome is established by the evidence available for this draft. A visible workflow does not demonstrate a financial result or a reduction in denials. Confirm product capabilities separately from any performance claim, and request relevant supporting evidence before using a result in your purchasing decision.
